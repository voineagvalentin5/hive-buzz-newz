France & Europe

Developing

France: thinner student marches on Nov 26; a Christmas tree is set alight near the Panthéon

Students and university staff marched in several French cities on Nov 26 at the call of an inter-union group and the Interfac collective. A franceinfo journalist counted about 2,000 marchers in Paris, AFP described "a few thousand" and said the Paris ranks were much thinner than in previous weeks, and only a few dozen gathered in Bordeaux. The higher-education ministry said 16% of institutions were disrupted, down from 20% to 25% in recent days, while an AEF info count found 28 of 57 universities it could check disrupted. franceinfo said the Paris march ended calmly, but Le JDD reports that a group broke away from the route and set fire to a Christmas tree on Place du Panthéon before riot police dispersed them. Higher-education minister Philippe Baptiste refused to cancel exams or grant a blanket "10 améliorable" pass mark and called for the blockades to stop, and youth organisations called a new march for Saturday, Nov 28, with one student telling AFP that many were saving themselves for Saturday and for Tuesday, Dec 1. This check found no new statement by Emmanuel Macron on the protests.

Sources: franceinfo (French) · AFP, on Midi Libre (French) · Le JDD (French)

Confirmed

France: police rally outside stations for a bigger risk allowance

Police officers gathered outside stations across France on Nov 26, with some Paris rallies held overnight, at the call of the Alliance, Unsa Police and Un1té unions, which want the special police allowance (ISSP) raised from 28.5% to 35% of base pay. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez offered 33.5% on Nov 13, phased in over three years to 2029, which the unions called insufficient; "It's 35% or nothing," Alliance's Fabien Vanhemelryck said in Lyon. Ouest-France counted about 100 officers in Rennes, about 60 in Lorient and about 20 in Argentan, franceinfo reported about 60 in Mulhouse, and Alliance also staged an action at the Hendaye border crossing with Spain.

Sources: Ouest-France (French) · AFP, on France 3 Régions (French) · franceinfo (French)

Confirmed

EU summit ends a day early without a budget deal; talks move to Dec 17-18

EU leaders broke off their Brussels summit on the 2028-2034 budget after about 12 hours of talks on Nov 26, cancelling the planned second day; the BBC reports four countries that want a smaller budget, including Denmark and Austria, walked out after several hours, and the Guardian says talks broke down shortly after midnight. Leaders will try again at a summit on Dec 17-18. Council president António Costa said 24 member states backed a €45bn loan to Ukraine for 2027, with Bulgaria joining only the non-military part, while Germany's Friedrich Merz said the loan must have the support of all 27. Emmanuel Macron called cutting long-term spending a "strategic mistake" and, according to DW, said a deal after this year would be too close to France's April election. Leaders also named Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next European Central Bank president.

Sources: BBC · DW · The Guardian (live)

Developing

Belgium: De Wever calls coalition leaders back on Monday; Vooruit's attendance uncertain

Prime Minister Bart De Wever has invited the leaders of his five coalition parties to a budget meeting on Monday, Nov 30, at 14:00 local time (8 am ET), RTL Info and Belga report, citing government sources. Vooruit leader Conner Rousseau says his party will only take part if it gets guarantees that automatic wage and benefit indexation stays and that a millionaires' tax is introduced; VRT says such guarantees look extremely unlikely after MR leader Georges-Louis Bouchez told parliament on Nov 26 that he opposed a tax rise for millionaires and multinationals.

Sources: VRT NWS · RTL Info / Belga (French)

Developing

Spain: final debate dominated by housing and a row over Feijóo's "calladita" remark

Pedro Sánchez, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, Santiago Abascal and Ada Colau met in the last televised debate on Nov 26 before Sunday's election, with housing dominating, the BBC reports. Feijóo told Colau "y usted calladita" over the Valencia floods, which Sánchez and Colau called sexist; Feijóo said after the debate he took it back if it had offended, but El País reports he repeated the term on arriving at the PP's Génova headquarters. On Nov 27 Sánchez told radio SER "I can't conceive of us losing," although nearly all polls published before the legal ban showed an advantage for a possible PP-Vox coalition, Reuters reports via the Guardian.

Sources: BBC · El País (Spanish) · The Guardian (live)

Developing

Albania: court suspends road-blocking fines as parliament tightens protest rules before Nov 28

Albania's Constitutional Court on Nov 26 partly accepted a complaint by student groups and associations and suspended the government decree's fine of 5,000 new lek for blocking roads, which Tirana News says was used in about 330 fines so far, while declining to suspend the other provisions until its final ruling. Exit News reports the Socialist majority approved the restrictions in parliament the same day, giving police power to restrict protests near public buildings for up to three days at a time and setting fines of 10,000 to 500,000 lek. Students and opposition leader Sali Berisha have called a large anti-government protest in Tirana on Nov 28, Albania's independence day.

Sources: Tirana News (Albanian) · Exit News · Albanian Telegraphic Agency

Confirmed

Serbia: Dec 27 vote formally called; students name academic Radomir Saičić as their candidate

Parliament speaker and acting president Aleksandar Čupić signed the decision on Nov 26 calling the presidential election for Dec 27, NIN reports. On Nov 27 the student movement, running as the citizens' group "Students Win Again", named chemistry professor and Serbian Academy member Radomir Saičić as its candidate, RTS reports; Saičić said it was time to "restore dignity" to the presidency. Branimir Nestorović, Branko Pavlović and Bogoljub Karić have also announced bids. IZLAZ reports the Serbian Progressive Party says it will boycott, while NIN reports Aleksandar Vučić said he would personally ignore the election and party bodies would decide.

Sources: NIN (Serbian) · RTS (Serbian) · IZLAZ

Developing

Ukraine: Russian drones kill six and cut power in six regions; Zelenskyy warns of a mass strike

Russian attacks killed six people and injured 25 across Ukraine over the past day, local authorities said on Nov 27; Russia launched 155 drones overnight, of which 110 were downed or suppressed, and strikes on substations temporarily left consumers in six oblasts without power, with Dnipropetrovsk and Kherson worst hit, the Kyiv Independent reports. Zelenskyy said on Nov 26 that Russia was preparing a new mass strike. Ukraine's general staff said its forces hit a Transneft oil facility in Bashkortostan and the Marinovka airfield in Volgograd overnight into Nov 26, and military intelligence said drones hit the Slavyansk-ECO refinery in Krasnodar; the Kyiv Independent could not independently verify the strikes. Donald Trump said on Nov 26 he had asked Vladimir Putin to "pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe", the Guardian reports.

Sources: Kyiv Independent · Kyiv Independent · The Guardian

Confirmed

Latvia: NATO jets scrambled after a drone briefly enters its airspace

An unidentified drone briefly flew over Latvia's Ludza municipality near the Russian border overnight into Nov 27, Latvia's armed forces said; an orange air-threat alert was issued at 2:30 local time (7:30 pm ET on Nov 26) and lifted at 2:55. Fighter jets from NATO's Baltic air-policing mission were scrambled and a Latvian interceptor drone was deployed. The army did not say where the drone came from and has sent extra air-defence units to the eastern border, Euronews reports.

Sources: Euronews · The Guardian (live)

Middle East

Developing

Kuwait: drone strike kills two Italian technicians; Kuwait and Italy blame Iran, which denies it

Two Italian civilians working for Germany's Rheinmetall, named by ANSA as Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, were killed on Nov 26 when drones hit a site in Kuwait where they were maintaining Skyguard air-defence antennas; Kuwait's defence ministry had earlier reported four people hurt by falling shrapnel, including a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national. Kuwait called it "heinous Iranian aggression", and Italy's Antonio Tajani said several Iranian drones struck Kuwaiti military sites around 14:00 local time (6 am ET). Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi denied responsibility, called the incident "suspicious" and offered a joint investigation, Euronews reports, while Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar and the UAE condemned the attack.

Sources: BBC · Euronews · ANSA

Developing

Hormuz: Iran's Guards claim more tankers hit mines and say warning fire turned back a US F-16

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Nov 26 that two tankers, one a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, exploded and caught fire after hitting mines north of Oman's Khasab port on an "unauthorised" route; Reuters, via Arab News, notes maritime agencies tend to report fewer incidents than the Guards, and UKMTO reported an explosion from an unknown projectile near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai that day. On Nov 27 the Guards said another tanker hit a mine north of Oman's Ghanam Island and a second turned back, Iran International reports. The Guards also said on Nov 27 that their air defences fired warning shots at a US F-16 escorting vessels near Hengam Island, and Anadolu reported no immediate US response. The cited reports do not name the vessels, and none of them independently confirms the mine strikes.

Sources: Reuters, on Arab News · Iran International · Anadolu

Confirmed

Saudi Arabia: Houthi strike on the Jazan refinery kills a Nepali worker

A "hostile aerial projectile" hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on Nov 26, killing a Nepali worker, injuring several others and temporarily halting some operations, Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said; the refinery can process up to 400,000 barrels a day. The Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis and later said it had destroyed the ballistic-missile launch platform in Yemen's Hodeidah governorate used in the attack. The coalition also said it intercepted two ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh on Nov 26, The National reports.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Arab News · The National

Developing

Gaza: Israeli strikes kill a Hamas official and his wife; tolls range from eight to ten

Israeli strikes across Gaza on Nov 26 killed Khaled Abu Nada, whom the BBC describes as Hamas's deputy education minister, and his wife in an apartment in Deir al-Balah; Netanyahu confirmed his death and said about 80 Hamas officials had been killed in the last month. The BBC, citing Hamas's civil defence, reports eight people killed in three strikes, while Al Jazeera, citing health officials, reports at least 10. Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya said the group would discuss with other factions how to respond to what he called Israel's daily killing, according to Reuters via Al Jazeera.

Sources: BBC · Al Jazeera

Africa

Developing

Sudan: drone strike on a passenger truck kills 11 in West Kordofan

A drone hit a truck carrying passengers and gum arabic in Ghubeish, an RSF-held town in West Kordofan, on Nov 26, killing at least 11 people, a hospital source told AFP; the source said nine were wounded, while the RSF-allied TASIS alliance put the wounded at 14. TASIS blamed the Sudanese army, which The Sudan Times says could not be independently established, and no army response was reported.

Sources: AFP, on Arab News · The Sudan Times

Confirmed

Nigeria: gunmen open fire near Peter Obi's rally in Benin City

Three gunmen in a pickup truck opened fire near Benin airport during presidential candidate Peter Obi's NDC rally in Edo State on Wednesday, Nov 25, wounding one man in the thighs, Edo police said; police are hunting the suspects. Premium Times notes it came days after gunmen killed six people, including a former state lawmaker, at an NDC rally in Delta State, and The Guardian Nigeria reports the House of Representatives urged President Tinubu to consider emergency powers if Delta's security worsens.

Sources: Premium Times · The Guardian Nigeria

Asia-Pacific

Confirmed

Thailand: Bangkok refinery fire injures nine; all units ordered shut

An explosion and fire at Bangchak's Phra Khanong refinery in Bangkok at about 14:45 local time on Nov 26 (2:45 am ET) injured nine people, three critically according to AFP, the BBC reports; the company said the fire was out within 30 minutes. Thailand's energy minister said it started in a distillation tower, while The Nation reports it was confined to the hydrocracking unit. The industry ministry has ordered all four refining units to stay shut until a joint safety inspection, and officials detected volatile organic compounds at one of four air-testing points.

Sources: BBC · The Nation (Thailand)

Confirmed

China tells Japan to stop "stirring up trouble" in the South China Sea

China's defence ministry spokesperson Chen Xi said on Nov 26 that Japan should stop "stirring up trouble" over the South China Sea, after Tokyo agreed to supply the Philippines with coastal radars and surveillance drones under a 4.3 billion yen grant signed on Nov 9. Philstar reports Chen also called Japan "a real destroyer of peace and tranquility" in the Asia-Pacific and criticised the Philippines' Nov 15 defence pact with Australia without naming Australia.

Sources: Reuters, on The Straits Times · Philstar

Latin America

Confirmed

Brazil: Lula picks Bradesco chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco as finance minister

Brazil's presidency announced on Nov 26 that Luiz Carlos Trabuco, 75, chairman of Bradesco's board, accepted President Lula's invitation to run the finance ministry from 2027; G1 says he is the first confirmed name of Lula's new government. AFP reports the São Paulo stock exchange closed up 6% on Thursday as rumours of the appointment spread, after the real briefly crossed six to the dollar on Monday, and that the choice drew criticism from a left-wing lawmaker and from defeated rival Flávio Bolsonaro.

Sources: G1 (Portuguese) · AFP

US & Canada

Confirmed

Thanksgiving storm cuts power to nearly 1 million and disrupts flights

A powerful storm cut power to nearly 1 million US customers at its peak on Nov 26, with more than 908,000 still out at 9:56 pm ET according to PowerOutage.us, as gusts of up to 70 mph hit the East Coast; FlightAware counted 454 cancellations and 2,411 delays, Reuters reports. New Jersey was under a state of emergency, coastal flooding hit towns from North Carolina to New Jersey, and Macy's 100th parade in New York was moved to Saturday for the first time in its history, the Guardian reports. On the West Coast, king tides on Nov 25 tied or broke all-time high-tide records in Los Angeles and Santa Barbara, according to the National Weather Service.

Sources: Reuters, on Investing.com · The Guardian

Markets & Business

Developing

Oil heads for its first weekly gain in four weeks; US stocks reopen for a half day

Brent crude rose 73 cents, or 0.8%, to $97.30 a barrel by 0935 GMT (4:35 am ET) on Nov 27 after touching its highest since Nov 9, and US WTI was at $85.85, up 2.6% from Wednesday as there was no Thursday settlement, Reuters reports; Yahoo Finance put Brent near $92 in its morning update. Reuters cites attacks on tankers near Hormuz and on Saudi facilities, and reports OPEC+ has agreed in principle to keep output targets for at least the first quarter of 2027 at Sunday's meeting. US markets were closed for Thanksgiving and close early at 1 pm ET on Friday; Yahoo Finance reported Dow futures up 1%, S&P 500 futures up 1.7% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 2%.

Sources: Reuters, on Investing.com · CNBC · Yahoo Finance

Developing

Nvidia reportedly halts server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported on Nov 26, after new US export-licence rules took effect last week; it was unclear whether the halt is temporary, and Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro did not immediately comment, Reuters reports. Malay Mail, citing the FT, says regional cloud providers have a 90-day window to secure licences, with one executive describing "sheer chaos in the region".

Sources: Reuters, on Investing.com · Malay Mail

Climate & Disasters

Developing

Vietnam orders evacuations as up to 600mm of rain is forecast for the central coast

Vietnam's government has told seven provinces and cities from Huế to Lâm Đồng to move people out of areas at risk of floods and landslides, under a directive signed on Nov 26 by Deputy Prime Minister Hồ Quốc Dũng. Forecasters expect 200mm to 400mm of rain from Nov 27 to 30 along the coast from Huế to Khánh Hòa, and more than 600mm in places. The new spell follows heavy rain on Nov 25-26, when Lý Sơn island recorded 404mm in 24 hours, Viet Nam News reports.

Sources: Viet Nam News · DTiNews
