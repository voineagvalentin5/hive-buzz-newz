France & Europe

Developing

France: sixth Saturday of cost-of-living protests, with the Yellow Vests joining the union march in Paris

Unions, youth groups and the Yellow Vests called a sixth straight Saturday of protests on Nov 21; France 24 says the anger has spread across Europe but is starting to run out of steam in France. Paris police approved one route, from République to Nation starting at 2 pm local time (8 am ET), and refused the Yellow Vests' requested marches to the Concorde and the Champs-Élysées, so they say they will walk the same route. Marches against violence toward women also begin in about a dozen French towns.

Sources: France 24 (live, French) · Sortiraparis

Confirmed

France adopts law presuming police acted lawfully when they fire their weapons

On Nov 20 the Senate approved the bill 205 votes to 110, completing its passage after the lower house voted in July. The government says it ends automatic custody for officers who use their guns, while rights groups call it a 'licence to kill', and the left plans to challenge it at the Constitutional Council.

Sources: France 24 · Public Sénat (French)

Confirmed

France: three police officers convicted of racist violence over the 2020 beating of Michel Zecler

A Paris court found the three officers guilty of intentional violence with racism as an aggravating factor, and of falsifying a police report. They were sentenced to between one and two years in prison, with further time suspended, and banned for life from the police.

Sources: The Guardian · BBC

Confirmed

Youth protests against 'militarisation' and the cost of living sweep European cities

Tens of thousands of people marched on Nov 20 in cities from Milan to London, Amsterdam and Lausanne, according to France 24, against military build-ups and for young people's future. In Berlin about a thousand young people protested mainly against German rearmament and a possible return of compulsory military service.

Sources: France 24 (live, French)

Confirmed

UK and EU seal biggest reset since Brexit, but a tuition-fee row could still derail it

At a Brussels summit on Nov 20, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders agreed deals on food and drink trade, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme letting 100,000 young Britons live, work and study in the EU, with the same access for Europeans in the UK. The Guardian reports the EU has threatened to block the deals within six months unless EU students pay domestic fees at British universities; Burnham says 'the deal is done'.

Sources: The Guardian · GOV.UK

Confirmed

Poland: Russian cruise missile lands near Western military drills; Warsaw calls it a provocation

Poland says a Kh-101 cruise missile, another suspected missile and a jet-powered drone entered its airspace early on Nov 20 during a huge Russian attack on Ukraine. The missile hit a rural area about 50 km from the Ukrainian border; no one was hurt. Prime Minister Tusk said it did not look like an accident, as a UK- and France-led exercise was under way nearby, and NATO scrambled jets.

Sources: BBC · AFP

Confirmed

Ukraine: Russia's biggest attack of the year; Zelensky calls Putin a 'killer'

Russia struck bridges in Kyiv and rail stations in western Ukraine on Nov 20 with 110 missiles and more than 400 drones, the BBC reports, killing at least nine; Reuters cited Ukrainian officials saying eight died. France has asked for a UN Security Council meeting after the Dutch and Austrian embassies in Kyiv were damaged. Zelensky told CNN Putin is 'a killer' after Trump called him a friend, and overnight strikes on Kryvyi Rih killed two, including a boy.

Sources: BBC · Reuters (via The Straits Times)

Confirmed

Denmark expels Russia's defence attaché over a plot to kill a US-Ukrainian rapper

Danish intelligence says Russian intelligence planned an attempt to kill the rapper Kyivstoner before a Sept 25 concert near Copenhagen, which police stopped by arresting three men. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said the attaché must leave within seven days and that Denmark is pushing to halt non-essential EU visas for Russians.

Sources: The Guardian · PET (Danish Security and Intelligence Service) · Euronews

Confirmed

Spain: Sánchez admits 'mistakes' in his TV debate with Feijóo

Opening the Nov 19 head-to-head debate, ten days before the Nov 29 election, Pedro Sánchez said his government 'has not been perfect' but defended Spain's economic record. Alberto Núñez Feijóo attacked him using criticism from Catalan separatist Carles Puigdemont, saying Spain deserves 'a decent president'.

Sources: DW (Spanish) · ABC (Spanish)

Confirmed

Albania: police push protesters off Tirana's main boulevard on the 174th day of protests

On Nov 20 police again kept protesters from rallying on the boulevard outside the prime minister's office, citing a government act that bars gatherings that block traffic. After scuffles, demonstrators marched through Tirana and called for a big national protest on Nov 28.

Sources: ATA · Vox News Albania

Middle East

Developing

Hormuz: tankers hit near Oman's Khasab as Iran says one struck a mine

The UK maritime agency UKMTO says two oil tankers were hit by 'unknown projectiles' north and east of Khasab, with no injuries reported, Al Jazeera says. Iran's navy says a tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine in a restricted area; it is not clear whether this is one of the same ships.

Sources: Al Jazeera (live) · Anadolu Agency

Developing

Iran claims two US drones shot down over Hormuz as the US adds sanctions

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Nov 21 they downed a US MQ-9, which they call the 58th, and later an MQ-1 over Qeshm Island; the US military had not commented. Al Jazeera also reports new US sanctions on four Iranians Washington says acted as 'oil trustees', while efforts to end the war remain stalled.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Middle East Eye

Confirmed

Saudi-led coalition intercepts three Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh

The coalition said it shot down three ballistic missiles early on Nov 21, the third day in a row of emergency alerts in the Saudi capital, two days after a Houthi strike damaged Riyadh's tallest tower. The coalition also said it struck Houthi-held Hodeidah port and targets in Sanaa.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Arab News

Confirmed

Gaza: Israeli drone strikes kill seven in central Gaza despite the ceasefire

Medical sources told Al Jazeera that four people were killed by a drone strike in Bureij camp on Nov 20, two near Deir el-Balah and one in Nuseirat. Israel's army also carried out demolitions in Gaza City's Shujaiya area and east of Khan Younis.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Anadolu Agency

Confirmed

Iraq: fire at Basra's Hartha power station kills four

At least four people were killed and 35 injured when fire broke out at the 800-megawatt station and spread to nearby homes. Civil defence brought it under control and the cause is not yet known.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Anadolu Agency

After local elections, the ANC brought together more than 10 parties, including ActionSA, the EFF and MK, in a deal that would make ActionSA's Herman Mashaba mayor of Johannesburg and keep Nasiphi Moya in Tshwane, with the ANC naming Ekurhuleni's mayor. Police say an overcrowded boat headed to a burial capsized on Nov 20 after a mechanical failure, killing two adults and two children; 24 people were rescued. Residents told Reuters at least 300 were aboard, and the search continues.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Punch
