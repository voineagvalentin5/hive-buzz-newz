France & Europe

Developing

Belgium: national strike grounds flights as De Wever goes back to the king without a budget deal

Belgium's three main unions held a national demonstration in Brussels on Nov 23, with strikes across several sectors; Brussels police counted about 50,000 marchers and the unions about 70,000, according to VRT, which described the march as fairly calm. Air traffic controllers' walkout halted all flights at Brussels Airport from 07:00 to 21:00 local time and at Charleroi, and Brussels metro and train services were badly disrupted. A morning meeting of senior ministers ended without agreement; MR's David Clarinval said there was no deal, and VRT reports Vooruit has not accepted De Wever's new proposal. The palace said after De Wever met King Philippe that no decisive progress had been made but the parties would keep negotiating.

Sources: BBC · VRT NWS (Dutch, live) · Reuters, on Devdiscourse · POLITICO Europe

Developing

France: education minister says 98 pupils and about 30 staff were hurt in a week of school-protest violence

Education Minister Edouard Geffray told LCI on Nov 23 that 98 pupils and around 30 school staff were injured last week in violence on the fringes of the high-school protest movement, and that 280 staff have been hurt since it began; he mentioned firework-mortar fire and crowd panics and said those responsible would face criminal penalties. A Nov 13 government instruction to rectors and prefects had already asked for an early-morning police presence at blockaded schools, systematic arrests of troublemakers and disciplinary action. France 24 notes that parliament adopted a law on Nov 20 giving police more protection when using their weapons, and that an AFP count puts at least 31 people seriously injured by security forces since the protests began.

Sources: franceinfo (French) · Sud Ouest (French) · France 24

Developing

NATO allies meet on the Russian missiles that came down in Poland

The North Atlantic Council is meeting in Brussels on Nov 23 to discuss the objects that fell in south-eastern Poland on Nov 20, with Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski representing Poland. Accounts of what fell differ: the Lublin prosecutor's office said on Nov 22 that all three were Russian Kh-101 missiles (The Guardian), while earlier reports cited by the Kyiv Post said one was confirmed as a Kh-101 and the others were most likely a missile and a jet-powered drone. Poland has asked for more airborne early-warning aircraft and F-35s, but no new deployments have been announced, Stars and Stripes reports; no one was hurt.

Sources: The Guardian · Kyiv Post · Stars and Stripes

Developing

Ukraine: death toll from the Dymer apartment strike rises to at least 11

Kyiv regional official Tymur Tkachenko said at least 11 people were killed when a Russian drone hit a five-storey block in Dymer, north of Kyiv, on Nov 22; the dead include a teenager, and rescuers pulled a child and a 79-year-old woman out alive. The Guardian says overnight attacks killed at least 14 people across the Kyiv region, including three in a strike on a supermarket in Vyshneve (RFE/RL earlier reported two dead there). The Kyiv region declared Nov 23 a day of mourning.

Sources: The Guardian · RFE/RL

Confirmed

Zelensky says Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian corvette in Libya

Zelensky said seven Ukrainian naval drones sank the corvette Merkury at the Benghazi naval base on Nov 22 and destroyed a Ka-27 helicopter, and that a Russian container ship, the MV Angara, was also hit; satellite images described by the BBC show the corvette capsized with only its bow visible. Libya's eastern government condemned the attack, the Kremlin declined to comment, and the UN mission in Libya called for an independent investigation. A day earlier, a Russian cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad was hit by a drone in the Gulf of Finland; Finland's defence forces said Ukraine was likely behind it.

Sources: BBC · The Guardian · RFE/RL

Middle East

Developing

Hormuz: IRGC says two more supertankers hit mines; UKMTO reports a separate tanker incident

In a statement late on Nov 22, the IRGC Navy said two supertankers using 'unauthorised routes' were stopped by mine explosions north of Ghanem Island and east of the Strait of Hormuz; it did not name the ships. UKMTO separately reported an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, and ABC News says it is not confirmed whether the two reports are the same. CNN data cited by The Jerusalem Post show at least 22 attacks reported to UKMTO in November as of Sunday, after more than 30 in October.

Sources: ABC News (live) · Anadolu (citing Fars/IRGC) · The Jerusalem Post

Confirmed

Iran vows to use 'all available means' against the US blockade; Trump floats strikes after the new year

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said on Nov 23 that Iran would use all means at its disposal against the US naval blockade, and that security in the Strait of Hormuz could be restored quickly if Washington lifted sanctions and the blockade and met the terms of June's memorandum of understanding. In comments to Fox News on Nov 22, Trump said he was inclined to order strikes after the new year if no agreement is reached, calling Iran's negotiators 'professional tap dancers'.

Sources: CBS News (live) · ABC News (live) · Fox News

Confirmed

Gaza: Oxfam report finds signs of possible contamination across more than 99% of the territory

A report published on Nov 23 by Oxfam and the Conflict and Environment Observatory says at least one potential contamination source from the war is present across more than 99% of Gaza, affecting air, soil, water and food. It cites UN estimates of 61 million tonnes of debris and says Gaza's water supply in April 2026 was still 61% below pre-war levels; Oxfam calls for an independent investigation.

Sources: Al Jazeera · Oxfam International · RFI (French)

Africa

Developing

Sudan: drone attacks around Khartoum damage a UN building

Drones struck Khartoum and nearby areas between about 2 am and 4:30 am local time on Nov 22, damaging a UN building while staff were inside; UN coordinator Denise Brown called the risk to staff 'unacceptable'. Al Jazeera, blaming the RSF, reports four people killed, three of them in a home in northern Omdurman, while DW said there were no immediate reports of casualties from the strike it described. A Sudanese army source said the drones were launched from Ethiopia, which could not be verified; Ethiopia has denied such accusations before.

Sources: Al Jazeera · DW

Confirmed

Nigeria: 29 dead after a boat carrying mourners capsizes in Bayelsa

A boat carrying people to a funeral capsized in Bayelsa State on Nov 20 after developing mechanical problems; Nigeria's emergency agency told the BBC that 29 bodies had been recovered and 50 people rescued. The government ordered stricter enforcement of safety rules on inland waterways.

Sources: BBC · Channels Television

Asia-Pacific

Confirmed

India and Nepal sign eight agreements as Modi announces a development package

After talks with Nepal's PM Balendra Shah in New Delhi on Nov 23, Narendra Modi announced a development package of 24,000 crore Nepalese rupees (₹15,000 crore) for Nepal's reconstruction, and the two sides signed eight memorandums covering areas including policing, health, space and urban development. They also launched a weekly Ayodhya–Janakpur train, with regular service due to start on Nov 28.

Sources: Hindustan Times · CNBC-TV18

Latin America

Confirmed

Mexico captures cartel leader 'El Chapo Isidro' in a deadly operation

Mexican special forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, one of the FBI's ten most wanted fugitives, in Guasave, Sinaloa, on the night of Nov 20; the army said seven suspected attackers and four soldiers were killed. The military said the operation followed coordination with US intelligence, and roadblocks were set up in parts of Sinaloa afterwards.

Sources: France 24 · US Department of Justice

Confirmed

Brazil: storm kills five and unroofs thousands of homes in Tupanciretã

A severe storm hit Tupanciretã in Rio Grande do Sul before dawn on Nov 20, killing five people and injuring 265, according to BBC Brasil (AFP: more than 250). BBC Brasil says more than 7,000 homes lost their roofs and AFP says about 8,000 were damaged; civil defence classed it as a supercell and did not rule out a tornado. The federal government recognised a state of calamity on Nov 21.

Sources: BBC News Brasil (Portuguese) · AFP

Climate & Disasters

Confirmed

COP31 ends without its main deal after the EU and others reject the text

The Antalya talks closed on Nov 22, the longest UN climate conference on record, without agreement on the main political text, which did not mention fossil fuels; the EU, Pacific island nations and others objected, and many poorer countries said it lacked clear adaptation finance. The BBC says this is the first such collapse since Copenhagen in 2009. UN chief António Guterres said he was 'deeply disappointed'; some outcomes were reached, including progress on a just transition mechanism, and COP32 is due in Ethiopia in November 2027.

Sources: BBC · UN News · GOV.UK
