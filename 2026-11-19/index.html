France & Europe

Confirmed

France: third public-sector strike draws smaller crowds, unions vow more action

France's Interior Ministry counted 146,000 marchers nationwide on Tuesday, Nov 17, including 28,000 in Paris, while unions claimed more than 500,000. That is well down from the 575,000 the ministry counted on Nov 5. Official strike rates were under 3% in each branch of the civil service. The union alliance said on Nov 18 that the prime minister's Nov 13 offers fell short and that it is preparing further action.

Sources: RFI · AFP · Ministère de l'Action et des Comptes publics · CGT État (union statement)

Confirmed

France confirms limited troop missions in Ukraine after RFI reveals the 'Volia' operation

RFI reported on Nov 16 that small French military teams have been training Ukrainian forces and maintaining French-supplied weapons inside Ukraine since at least the summer, and that parliament was never informed. On Nov 17, junior armed forces minister Alice Rufo told the National Assembly the missions are occasional, limited in number and far from the front, and that they are not a combat deployment requiring notice under Article 35 of the constitution.

Sources: RFI · Ouest-France

Developing

Europe-wide youth strike on Friday, with a general strike in Italy the same day

Youth and student groups have called strikes and walkouts in more than 25 European countries for Friday, Nov 20, over the cost of living, education, housing and militarisation. In Italy, the CGIL union's general strike that day excludes air and rail, but a national rail strike follows from Friday evening to Saturday evening, according to The Local.

Sources: The Local Italy · AFP · CWI (organiser call)

Developing

Belgium: King gives De Wever until Nov 23 to rescue the budget

King Philippe gave Prime Minister Bart De Wever another week on Nov 16 after weekend talks failed. Four of the five coalition parties accept the broad outline of a deal, but the left-leaning Vooruit wants high earners to pay more. De Wever is seeking 10 billion euros in savings by 2029.

Sources: AFP · BelgiumNow · RTBF

Developing

Serbia: new parliament sworn in, but the speaker vote keeps slipping

Serbia's new National Assembly was constituted on Nov 17 and its 250 members took the oath. The Student List nominated Aleksandar Cupic for speaker and the United Serbia group nominated Stojan Radenovic. No vote had taken place by Nov 18, and the session was adjourned to Thursday morning, Nov 19.

Sources: CRTA · Al Jazeera Balkans · Tanjug

Confirmed

Spain: polls still favour a PP-Vox majority 11 days before the vote

The 40dB daily tracker for El Pais put the PP at 32.2% and the Socialists at 30.7% on Nov 18, with Vox slipping to 17%. The tracker still gives the PP and Vox together an absolute majority. A PF Global poll for Politico had the PP and Socialists within the margin of error, and Politico says Sanchez and Feijoo face each other in a debate on Thursday night.

Sources: The Local Spain · Politico Europe · AS

Confirmed

Albania: Tirana protests reach day 172 after clashes at the PM's office

Student and citizen protesters marched from the Education Ministry to parliament on Nov 18, demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation. A day earlier, riot police broke up a protest outside the prime minister's office and detained several people.

Sources: Albanian Telegraphic Agency · Exit News

Confirmed

Ukraine brings down a road span of the Crimean Bridge, then strikes it again

A Ukrainian missile and drone operation on Nov 17 collapsed an arch and about 200 metres of the bridge's roadway into the Kerch Strait, stopping road traffic. Russia said a further drone attack early on Nov 19 temporarily halted rail traffic, with no one hurt. The Kremlin says the bridge will be rebuilt.

Sources: BBC · Al Jazeera · Kyiv Independent

Confirmed

Ukraine: Russian strikes on central Odesa kill four

Three Russian strikes hit Odesa's historic centre on the morning of Nov 18, killing four people and injuring nine, regional governor Oleh Kiper said. Zelensky said Russia struck the same site again after rescuers arrived. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 141 drones overnight and 126 were shot down or suppressed.

Sources: Kyiv Independent · Kyiv Post · Kyiv Independent (daily roundup)

Hormuz: more tankers hit, and Iran claims two supertankers struck mines

UKMTO said an unknown projectile hit a tanker's engine room six nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, at 11:45 am ET on Nov 18, and the crew are safe. ADNOC said one of its tankers was attacked on Nov 17, leaving one crew member missing and 11 injured, and the LPG tanker Tara Gas was still burning on Nov 18. On Nov 19, Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy claimed two supertankers hit mines and exploded on a 'restricted route'. That claim has not been independently confirmed.

A US official told AFP that American warplanes hit a Revolutionary Guards missile launch site on Qeshm Island on Nov 17 in self-defence. Earlier that day the Guards said they had targeted a US-operated radar at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa Air Base with drones. A local Iranian official said the strikes caused no damage to residential or commercial areas.

Sources: Arab News (AFP/Reuters) · Iran International

Confirmed

Iran: bus crash that killed 13 workers sparks anti-government protest in Abdanan

Hundreds of people marched in Abdanan, in Ilam province, on Nov 17 after a bus carrying oil and gas workers overturned on Nov 14, killing 13. The IRGC-linked Fars agency said crowds reached about 1,000 and turned political, and rights groups report several arrests, including a 15-year-old.

Sources: RFE/RL · Euronews · Hengaw

Confirmed

Gaza: Israel's first announced ground push beyond the Yellow Line since the ceasefire

Israeli tanks moved into an area east of Nuseirat camp on Nov 18 in what the military called a targeted operation against a tunnel, and families fled. Gaza's Civil Defence said three people were killed in separate drone strikes that day. Hamas called it a flagrant violation of the ceasefire. Israel's Cogat said on Nov 19 the operation had ended and Salah al-Din road had reopened.

Sources: BBC · Al Jazeera

Nvidia beats forecasts but its shares fall again

Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.84 billion and guided to $130 billion for the next quarter, both above analyst estimates. Its shares still fell 2.89% on Nov 18. The new board chair said former CEO Jensen Huang will not be reinstated.

Confirmed

IEA calls the Hormuz crisis the largest energy supply shock in history

The IEA's World Energy Outlook, published Nov 19, says the energy world will not return to how it was before. In a scenario with prolonged risk to shipping, oil flows needed through Hormuz could drop from 20 million barrels a day before the crisis to under 2 million by 2035 as countries build bypasses and switch fuels.

Sources: IEA · NBC News
