France & Europe

Source-supported reporting

France: Nov 5 strike draws hundreds of thousands; 448 arrested

Interior Ministry figures put Thursday's nationwide cost-of-living marches at 575,000 people (65,000 in Paris), while the CGT union claimed nearly 1.6 million. Officials counted 448 arrests, 53 injured officers and 86 fire incidents; most processions were peaceful, with clashes in Paris, Lyon, Rennes and Toulouse.

Sources: Anadolu Agency

France: Lecornu calls a Monday social conference with unions

After the strike, Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said a social conference with unions and employers will be held at Matignon on Monday. CGT leader Sophie Binet called the day extremely effective and pressed for wage rises, including a 10% hike in the minimum wage.

Sources: Anadolu Agency

Belgium: King Philippe rejects De Wever's resignation

King Philippe refused Prime Minister Bart De Wever's resignation on Friday and ordered him to restart coalition budget talks at once, with a progress report due Nov 16. The five-party majority has pledged concessions to close a roughly 10 billion euro gap by 2029.

Sources: Politico Europe

Unions are pressing ahead with Wednesday's 24-hour general strike over housing and living costs. Renfe drivers plan a full-day walkout on Nov 10 and partial stoppages on Nov 12, while Pedro Sanchez held a Valencia campaign rally on Friday ahead of the Nov 29 election.
